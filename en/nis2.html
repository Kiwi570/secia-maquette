Sheet 02.7 · Regulation

NIS2 compliance

The NIS2 Directive (EU 2022/2555) is the European cybersecurity law that strengthens the protection of critical and digital services. It widens the scope of the first NIS Directive, introduces stricter security and reporting rules, and makes management accountable for compliance.

In Belgium, it is transposed by the Act of 26 April 2024, in force since 18 October 2024. The competent authority is the Centre for Cybersecurity Belgium (CCB).

Scope

Does NIS2 apply to you?

NIS2 applies to medium-sized and large entities in 18 sectors: 11 sectors of high criticality (Annex I) and 7 other critical sectors (Annex II). Member States had to identify essential and important entities by 17 April 2025, then update that list at least every two years.

Sectors of high criticality (Annex I) 11

  1. Energy
  2. Transport
  3. Banking
  4. Financial market infrastructures
  5. Health
  6. Drinking water
  7. Waste water
  8. Digital infrastructure
  9. ICT service management (B2B)
  10. Public administration
  11. Space

Other critical sectors (Annex II) 7

  1. Postal and courier services
  2. Waste management
  3. Chemicals
  4. Food
  5. Manufacturing (including medical devices)
  6. Digital providers
  7. Research

The ten measures of Article 21

Where do you stand on the ten NIS2 measures?

Penalties

What non-compliance costs.

Essential entities

€10M

or 2% of total worldwide annual turnover

Important entities

€7M

or 1.4% of total worldwide annual turnover

Jurisdictional complexities

  • Entities fall under the jurisdiction of each Member State in which they provide services.
  • Organisations operating in several countries are supervised by all competent authorities.
  • Entities that depend on activities located outside the EU must ensure their EU operations remain functional if those services are disrupted.

Maximum administrative fines, whichever amount is higher.
